Quorum is a self-hosted multi-agent reasoning platform.

You give it a brief — a strategic decision, a research question, a policy
draft — and an optional set of source documents. Quorum then runs a
seven-stage pipeline that designs a topic-specific knowledge graph,
instantiates one AI agent per real-world entity in that graph, runs them
through a structured debate, and writes you a prediction report.

The whole stack runs locally with the LLM provider of your choice (Google
Gemini, Anthropic Claude, or Azure AI Foundry). There is no managed service
and no hosted dependency you need to sign up for.

## Why Quorum exists

Most LLM tools collapse multi-stakeholder reasoning into a single voice
optimising for a single answer. Real decisions need **structured
disagreement**: a risk officer thinks differently from an opportunity scout;
a constitutional historian sees what a startup founder misses. Quorum makes
that disagreement explicit, tracks it across rounds, and surfaces both the
consensus and the dissents in the final report.

The same pipeline works across domains without templates or rewrites:

- Product strategy and roadmap decisions
- Engineering architecture trade-offs
- Hiring and team-structure questions
- Policy impact analysis
- Research thesis stress-testing
- Market entry and investment decisions

You do not pick a domain. You describe the question, and the pipeline adapts.

## How it differs from a single LLM

A single LLM playing several "personas" tends to converge on its own bias —
every persona ends up sounding similar because they all share the same model
weights and the same conversational context. Quorum addresses this with
three structural mechanisms:

1. **Per-agent system prompts**, each derived from a real entity in the
   knowledge graph. The agent representing *Wuhan University* gets a
   different system prompt from *the Student Affairs Office*.
2. **Stance and bias tracking** across rounds. Each agent enters with a
   declared stance (`support` / `oppose` / `neutral`) and a stated bias.
   Disagreement is data, not noise.
3. **A consensus moderator** that summarises the swarm position and
   explicitly lists dissenting agents at the end of the run.
